Epistemological Problem

HOW DO WE KNOW SOMETHING IS TRUE?

In a world flooded with assumptions, claims, and illusions, how do we distinguish genuine knowledge (Pramā) from error?

01

The Question

You see smoke rising from a distant mountain peak. You cannot see fire directly. Is fire actually present?

02

The Observation

You isolate a visible mark (Hetu): smoke. This sign is observed directly at the locus (Pakṣa).

03

The Reasoning

You retrieve the invariable connection (Vyāpti): smoke never exists without fire, as established in a kitchen.

04

Valid Knowledge

You conclude with certainty (Nigamana) that fire exists on the hill. Anumāna turns unobserved into known.

WHAT IS ANUMĀNA IN SIMPLE WORDS?

Anumāna literally means "after-knowledge" (anu = after, māna = knowledge). You know something unobserved by processing what is observed.

STEP A

1. Observe Something

You notice a visible mark or sign (Hetu), like smoke rising in the distance.

STEP B

2. Connect to Known Relation

You recall the universal invariable relation (Vyāpti): wherever there is smoke, there is fire.

STEP C

3. Reach Unseen Conclusion

You conclude that fire (Sādhya) is present right now on the hill (Pakṣa).

Educational Note: This 3-step sequence is a modern simplified visualization. Classical Nyāya formalizes this process into a precise 5-member syllogism (Pañcāvayava) designed for convincing others in formal debate.

THE CONNECTION THAT MAKES INFERENCE WORK

Kitchen Case: Positive Concomitance (Anvaya)

In the kitchen, both smoke (Hetu) and fire (Sādhya) are directly observed together. This establishes that smoke does not exist in isolation from fire.

⚡ ASYMMETRY OF VYĀPTI

Smoke guarantees Fire: Wherever smoke, there fire (Valid).
Fire does NOT guarantee Smoke: Wherever fire, there smoke (Invalid - e.g. red hot iron ball).

Advanced Concept

3 TYPES OF VYĀPTI-BASED INFERENCE

Classical Nyāya classifies Anumāna by which type of concomitance examples are available.

01 Most Common

Anvayavyatireki

Uses both positive AND negative examples. The Hetu is present where Sādhya is (kitchen), and absent where Sādhya is absent (lake).

e.g., Smoke → Fire (kitchen ✓, lake ✗)
02 Positive Only

Kevalānvayī

Uses only positive examples. No negative case possible because the Sādhya is universal — it exists everywhere.

e.g., "Whatever exists is nameable" (no non-nameable thing exists)
03 Negative Only

Kevalavyatirekī

Uses only negative examples. The Hetu is established only via absence — wherever the Sādhya is absent, the Hetu is also absent.

e.g., "Whatever is not earthy has no natural fragrance" (no positive case of non-earthy fragrant thing)

Defective Reasoning

HETVĀBHĀSA: WHEN REASON FAILS

In Nyāya, a flawed reason is called Hetvābhāsa ("fallacy of reason"). Inspect how invalid arguments are diagnosed and dismantled.

01. SAVYABHICĀRA Irregular

Inconclusive Reason

The reason is present where the Sādhya exists, but also where it does NOT exist.

"The hill has fire because it is knowable."
02. VIRUDDHA Contradictory

Contradictory Reason

The reason actually proves the OPPOSITE of what was claimed.

"Sound is eternal because it is produced."
03. BĀDHITA Ousted

Perceptual Contradiction

The conclusion is directly contradicted by a stronger Pramāṇa (Perception).

"Fire is cold because it is a substance."
Savyabhicāra (सव्यभिचार) Click fallacy cards above to inspect

Why It Fails:

"Knowability" exists in fire, but also in water, rocks, and thoughts. Because it is everywhere, it fails to specify fire.

Academic Classification:

Anaikāntika (Inconclusive) — Breach of exclusive concomitance.

Reference

SANSKRIT GLOSSARY

Every key term used in Nyāya logic — defined clearly.

Anumāna अनुमान

Inference / After-knowledge. Knowledge derived by reasoning from an observed sign. The primary subject of Nyāya logic.

Pramāṇa प्रमाण

Valid Means of Knowledge. Nyāya recognises 4: Pratyakṣa, Anumāna, Upamāna, Śabda.

Pakṣa पक्ष

Locus / Subject. The place or entity about which an inference is made (e.g., "the hill").

Hetu हेतु

Reason / Inferential Mark. The observed sign that triggers inference (e.g., smoke → fire).

Sādhya साध्य

Inferable Property / Goal. The unobserved property to be established (e.g., fire on the hill).

Vyāpti व्याप्ति

Invariable Concomitance. The universal, unfailing relation between Hetu and Sādhya. The backbone of inference.

Dṛṣṭānta दृष्टान्त

Exemplar. The familiar undisputed example that demonstrates Vyāpti (e.g., "as in a kitchen").

Pratijñā प्रतिज्ञा

Proposition (Step 1). The initial claim to be established (e.g., "The hill has fire").

Udāharaṇa उदाहरण

Universal Example (Step 3). States the invariable rule backed by an undisputed example.

Upanaya उपनय

Application (Step 4). Applies the universal Vyāpti rule to the specific subject under examination.

Nigamana निगमन

Conclusion (Step 5). Restates the original proposition as now proven through evidence and universal law.

Hetvābhāsa हेत्वाभास

Fallacy of Reason. A pseudo-reason that appears valid but is actually defective (Savyabhicāra, Viruddha, Bādhita).

Pramā प्रमा

Correct / Valid Knowledge. Knowledge that accurately corresponds to reality — the ultimate goal of applying Pramāṇas.

About Nyayax & Academic Transparency

What is Nyayax?

Nyayax is an interactive educational web experience designed to make classical Nyāya logic and inference (Anumāna) accessible through visual manipulation and interactive simulation.

Why Was It Created?

Traditional philosophy texts often present formal logic in dense prose. Nyayax turns logical structures into something students can see, step through, test, and question.

Academic Disclaimer

"Nyayax is an educational visualization. Its inference engine simplifies selected Nyāya concepts for interactive learning and should not be treated as a complete formalization of the Nyāya philosophical system."
